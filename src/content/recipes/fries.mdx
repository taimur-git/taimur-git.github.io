## Notes

- To test potato starch content, dissolve 100g salt in 1 litre of water and drop them in. The ones that sink are starchy.
- Do not overdo the citric acid.
- Blend can keeps months in an airtight jar.
- If no thermometer, drop the handle of a wooden spoon into the oil.
  - At 160°C, small bubbles rise steadily and lazily around it.
  - At 190°C, the bubbles come fast and vigorously.
- **Small batches.** Too many chips at once drops the oil temperature and they absorb fat instead of
  crisping.
- **Two temperatures.** The low fry cooks the inside and the hot fry crisps the outside.
- After the first fry, chips keep a day in the fridge or 3 months frozen on a tray.
  Second fry straight from cold or frozen.
- Reuse the oil. Bin it when it smells sharp or foams up.
- If you need to cut further, use the blend on frozen chips. Skip the soak and dry the cut potatoes hard instead.

## Brined

- **A thin white film on the surface** is harmless. Skim it out.
- **Fuzzy mold means throw the whole batch away**.
- Keep everything under the brine, and never seal the jar.
- Use plain salt, not iodised.
- Or ferment in the fridge for 5 to 7 days.
- The brine is single use; throw it out.
- **ALTERNATIVE:** brine for **4 hours in the fridge** instead of 48 at room temperature. Fry it exactly as the recipe says, freeze included.

## Frozen

- Heat the oil to 190°C.
- Fry from frozen, in small batches, 3 to 4 minutes, until the bubbling dies down and they are
   golden and crisp.
- Salt and blend immediately.

Do not thaw them first. They go soggy and they spit.
