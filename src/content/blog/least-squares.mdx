Given a set of points, <Tooltip term="ordinary least squares">A method for fitting a line to data by minimizing the sum of squared vertical distances (residuals) from each point to the line. First derived independently by Legendre (1805) and Gauss (1809).</Tooltip> finds the line $\hat{y} = \beta x + \beta_0$ that minimizes the sum of **squared** vertical distances from each point to the line — the <Tooltip term="residuals">The vertical distance between each observed data point and the predicted value on the regression line. Residuals are what the model fails to explain.</Tooltip>.

<ManimRegression />

<Sidenote>Squaring residuals makes the loss function smooth and differentiable everywhere, so the minimum can be found analytically — no iterative optimization needed.</Sidenote>

The vertical grey segments are the residuals. The blue line is their least-squares solution. Drag a point far from the cluster and watch how much the line chases it. That sensitivity is not a bug — it is a direct consequence of squaring the residuals. A point twice as far contributes four times as much to the loss.[^gauss1809]

<Sidenote>Huber loss blends squared error (for small residuals) and absolute error (for large ones), controlled by a threshold δ. Points beyond δ are treated linearly instead of quadratically.</Sidenote>

This is why robust regression methods exist: <Tooltip term="Huber loss">A loss function that behaves like squared error for small residuals but switches to absolute error for large ones. This limits the influence of outliers compared to OLS.</Tooltip> and LAD regression replace the square with something that grows more slowly, so extreme points have less leverage over the fit.[^huber1964]

One thing the figure makes clear that algebra obscures: the line always passes through the centroid of the data — the point $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$. No matter where you drag the points, the line pivots around that center.[^stigler1981]

[^gauss1809]: Gauss, C.F. (1809). *Theoria Motus Corporum Coelestium*. Hamburg: Perthes & Besser.
[^huber1964]: Huber, P.J. (1964). Robust estimation of a location parameter. *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics*, 35(1), 73–101.
[^stigler1981]: Stigler, S.M. (1981). Gauss and the invention of least squares. *The Annals of Statistics*, 9(3), 465–474.
